## How a Refrigerator Works: A Detailed Explanation

A refrigerator is essentially a heat pump that moves heat from a cold area (the inside of the fridge) to a warmer area (the kitchen). It achieves this by circulating a special fluid called **refrigerant** through a closed loop, exploiting the physical property that certain fluids absorb heat when they change state (evaporate) and release heat when they change state (condense).

The entire process relies on four main components working in sequence: the **Compressor**, the **Condenser**, the **Expansion Valve**, and the **Evaporator**.

---

### 1. The Compressor (The Pump)

The compressor is the heart of the refrigeration system. Its job is to take the low-pressure, cool refrigerant gas that has just absorbed heat and pump it into a high-pressure, high-temperature gas.

*   **Function:** It acts as a pump, using mechanical energy (from the electricity) to compress the refrigerant vapor.
*   **Process:** As the gas is compressed, its pressure and temperature rise significantly. This high-pressure, hot gas is then routed to the condenser.
